Why Ethereum, why now.

  1. 1

    ECDSA is exposed.

    Once an Ethereum account sends a transaction, its public key is on-chain: the exposure a large quantum computer would target in ECDSA / secp256k1The elliptic-curve signature behind every Ethereum wallet today. A large quantum computer running Shor's algorithm could forge it from a public key..

    Source: ethereum.org
  2. 2

    Ethereum is moving to hashes.

    Ethereum's own researchers describe Hash-based signatureA signature whose only assumption is that a hash function is hard to invert. No curves, no lattices, the oldest post-quantum family. as the long-term foundation of a post-quantum Ethereum, with accounts switching through Account abstractionAccounts whose rules are code (ERC-4337, EIP-7702). Ethereum's roadmap sees it as the way accounts can switch to post-quantum signatures. A Hashmind vault already obeys only a hash-based signature..

    Source: Lean Ethereum, EF blog
  3. 3

    Hashmind agents already sign on Keccak.

    Every decision is signed with a WOTS (Winternitz one-time signature)A key made of hash chains that can sign exactly one message. Signing twice with it would leak it, so each one is spent once. from the agent's Merkle treeA tree of hashes whose single root commits to every leaf below it. An agent's root is registered on-chain; each leaf is one one-time key., over Keccak-256The hash function Ethereum runs everywhere (addresses, storage, events). Hash functions are expected to stay strong against quantum computers., and checked by its HookA contract that runs around each Uniswap v2 swap of a pair. On Hashmind it sets the fee, buys back and burns, inside hard limits. and vault.

    Source: HMS-1 spec
  4. 4

    What it unlocks.

    Agents that can hold a treasury for years, decisions nobody can forge, and a provenance that outlives Q-dayThe day a quantum computer can break today's elliptic-curve keys. Nobody can give a date; Ethereum researchers plan for it anyway., whenever it comes. Nobody knows when; that is why it is done now.

    Source: NIST on quantum and hashes
